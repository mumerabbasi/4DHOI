\begin{thesisabstract}
Generating a human who interacts convincingly with a real 3D scene is difficult because the input is semantic, whereas the output must satisfy geometric constraints. A hand must meet the correct object surface, a foot must land where the body is supported, and the body must neither float above the floor nor pass through nearby furniture. What separates a believable interaction from a merely photogenic one is correct contact with the reconstructed geometry. The most challenging part of establishing such contact is determining precisely, for each participating body part, which region of the scene surface it must touch.

Existing zero-shot approaches resolve this scene-side contact only partially: they either infer contact implicitly from a generated image or commit to a functional scene element in advance, thereby underdetermining the contact location on large surfaces. This thesis addresses that gap with a zero-shot method that synthesizes a single static human--scene interaction in the coordinate frame of a reconstructed scene. Given a natural-language instruction, a single posed RGB view, and the corresponding reconstructed ScanNet++ scene mesh, the method uses only pretrained foundation models, explicit camera geometry, and optimization, without any interaction-specific training.

The central idea is to use foundation models not to produce the final result, but to \emph{localize} where the interaction makes contact with the scene. The core contribution is an agentic contact-estimation procedure. An image-generation model proposes body-part-specific contact regions, which are extracted and composited onto the unmodified posed view to preserve exact correspondence with the calibrated camera. A vision-language evaluator then inspects each result against the intended interaction and issues corrections in a closed loop until the proposed contacts are accepted. This verify-and-correct loop transforms an unreliable single generation into precise, per-body-part contact regions, which are projected onto the mesh as 3D targets. Supporting this contribution, a lightweight interaction graph specifies the coarse contact structure, a monocular human-recovery model initializes the body, and a scene-aware optimization procedure refines the body pose to satisfy the contact targets while avoiding penetration and maintaining physical plausibility.

The method is evaluated on reconstructed ScanNet++ scenes using metrics of physical contact, scene penetration, interaction diversity, and image--text consistency, together with a vision-language-model evaluation of the rendered interaction. Together, the results show that grounding the interaction knowledge of foundation models in explicit, verified contacts enables the generation of geometrically plausible 3D human--scene interactions from text without task-specific training.
\end{thesisabstract}
