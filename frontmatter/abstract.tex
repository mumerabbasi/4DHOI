\begin{thesisabstract}

Reconstructed 3D scenes are empty: a gym is captured without anyone running on its treadmill. Placing a person into one, caught in the act, means getting three things right: the semantics, the right action on the right object; the physics, a plausible pose; and the contact. Foundation models readily supply the semantics, and the physics can be enforced on geometry. The contact is the hard part, especially its scene side: where exactly each body part should touch. Prior work leaves this open. Trained methods mark contact on the body, not the scene, and stay tied to their training scenes. Zero-shot methods read contact implicitly from a generated image, which struggles with complex interactions like stepping off a ladder, or pick a scene element, without saying where on it the contact falls.

This thesis presents a zero-shot method that turns a language instruction, a single posed RGB view, and the reconstructed scene mesh into a SMPL-X body in the scene's metric frame. It uses no interaction training data. Foundation models know how people use things well enough to supply the contact too, painting the regions on the scene view. But a single generation cannot be trusted: it may mark the wrong surface, miss a contact, or draw one the wrong size. The central contribution is an agentic loop that makes this reliable: an image model proposes the regions and a vision-language model checks them against the interaction, correcting until all are right. The accepted regions become 3D targets on the mesh, and a scene-aware optimization fits a monocularly initialized body to meet them while avoiding penetration.

On 23 interactions in 15 ScanNet++ scenes, this loop eliminates missed contacts and nearly halves the contact error of one shot. The full method places body parts more precisely than PhySIC, the closest baseline, and penetrates the scene less. Precise, verified contact, one region per body part, turns foundation-model knowledge into a physically grounded 3D human.

\end{thesisabstract}
